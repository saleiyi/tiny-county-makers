The four film formats

  • Classic Polaroid (3.5 x 4.2 in) is the original instant print: a portrait frame with the widest border and a near-square window.
  • Instax Mini (2.1 x 3.4 in) is the smallest and the most portrait, made for wallets, phone cases and party favours.
  • Instax Square (3.4 x 2.8 in) gives a square window with the border spread evenly around it.
  • Instax Wide (4.3 x 3.4 in) is the landscape one, good for groups and wide shots.

Frame colours and finishes

  • White is the standard instant film border and the safest pick for colour photos.
  • Cream and blush suit weddings, anniversaries and a warm palette.
  • Black makes the frame read like a darkroom print; the caption switches to white so it stays readable.
  • Kraft is a warm tan that looks like card stock rather than plastic.
  • Finishes are one blend each: Warm film warms the photo, Faded lifts the shadows, and Black and white desaturates it.

Printing a sheet of frames

  • Set the download to US Letter or A4 and the frames tile with a 4 mm gutter and a 1.27 cm margin.
  • A US Letter sheet holds 4 classic, 6 mini, 6 square or 2 wide frames; A4 holds 4 classic, 9 mini, 6 square or 3 wide frames.
  • Cut along the light dashed lines with a guillotine or a craft knife, and print at 100 percent with no scaling.
  • Matte card stock suits the cream, kraft and blush frames, while gloss keeps colour photos punchier.

Polaroid frame FAQ

How do I make a Polaroid frame?

Upload a photo, choose one of the four film formats and leave the download set to a single frame, then type a caption if you want one. The tool drops the photo into the film window and gives you a print-ready 300 DPI PNG of the whole frame, border and all, with no watermark and nothing uploaded.

Is the Polaroid frame maker free?

Yes. Uploading a photo, switching between the four film formats, changing the frame colour and the film finish, typing a caption, and downloading the frames as a 300 DPI PNG are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no limit, and the photo never leaves your device.

What is the difference between the Polaroid and Instax sizes?

The classic Polaroid is the biggest at 3.5 x 4.2 in, and its photo window is a near-square 7.9 x 7.9 cm. Instax Mini is the smallest and the most portrait at 2.1 x 3.4 in, Instax Square is 3.4 x 2.8 in, and Instax Wide is the landscape one at 4.3 x 3.4 in. Every format keeps the wide bottom border that instant film is known for.

Can I use my own photo with Instax film?

Yes. Upload any PNG, JPG or WebP up to 12 MB and the tool crops it to fill the photo window, so a shot where the subject sits near the middle reads best. The preview and the download are drawn from the same recipe, so what you see is exactly what prints.

How do I print a whole sheet of Polaroid frames?

Set the download to a US Letter or A4 sheet and the tool tiles as many frames as will fit with a light gutter between them. A Letter sheet takes four classic frames, six mini frames or two wide frames, and the PNG comes out at the full 2550 x 3300 px so you can trim the frames apart.

What do the film finishes do?

Original colours leaves the photo untouched. Warm film adds a gentle orange cast, Faded lifts the shadows for a slightly washed-out look, and Black and white desaturates the photo. Each finish is one canvas blend, so the preview and the download always match.
