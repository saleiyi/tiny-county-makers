Choosing a card shape

  • Arch is the rounded-top sign that reads as modern wedding: it matches acrylic arch signs and arch menus.
  • Straight rectangle is the classic card and the easiest to trim by hand or fit into a plain holder.
  • Rounded corners soften the rectangle without the height of an arch, which suits informal and garden weddings.

Choosing the card colour

  • White is the safest choice for colour photos and the standard lab stock.
  • Ivory suits warm palettes and reads softer than pure white in photographs.
  • Blush picks up soft pink and rose palettes without competing with the flowers.
  • Sage works with greenery, dried flowers and a lot of modern wedding schemes.
  • Black turns the number white and gives a bold, evening look; it is the one to avoid on a home inkjet.
  • Any other colour is fine too: the swatch picker takes any hex value and the ink flips automatically at mid grey.

What the download contains

  • A flat 300 DPI PNG of the finished card, card stock included, at the exact print size you chose.
  • The number, and the optional line of names, both shrunk to fit so long names never clip.
  • An optional photo cropped to fill the card, with the number on a band of paper beneath it.
  • No watermark, no logo and no signup, so the file goes straight to a lab or a home printer.

Printing tips

  • Ask for a borderless print at the size you chose, or print on 4 x 6 in and 5 x 7 in photo paper at 100 percent.
  • Card stock over 200 gsm stands up on its own; ordinary photo paper is better off in a holder.
  • Print one card first and check the number against the table plan before you print the whole set.
  • Matte paper suits black, ivory and sage cards; gloss keeps photo cards punchier.
  • Want a matching extra? The same photo works in the photo strip maker or as a photo keychain favour.

Questions

Table number FAQ

How do I make a table number?

Type the number, add the couple's names if you want them, choose the card shape and the paper colour, then download a print-ready 300 DPI PNG. You can add a photo as well: it runs across the whole card and the number sits on a band of paper below, so it stays readable.

Is the table number maker free?

Yes. Typing the numbers, adding a photo and the names, changing the shape, the paper colour and the size, and downloading the PNG are all free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many cards you make. Everything runs in your browser, so your photos are never uploaded to us.

What size is a wedding table number?

The most common wedding table number is a 4 x 6 in card, about 10 x 15 cm, and 5 x 7 in is the usual step up for a larger table. The tool also offers A5, which frames nicely. Every size downloads as a 300 DPI PNG at the exact print size you picked.

Can I add photos to my table numbers?

Yes. Add one photo and it fills the card, with the number printed on a band of card stock underneath. Photo table numbers are popular with childhood pictures of the couple, a shot of the venue, or the dog, the cat or the place they met.

Can I print table numbers at home?

A home printer is fine on 4 x 6 in or 5 x 7 in photo paper, and a print lab gives a sharper result on card stock. Print at 100 percent, or fit to the paper size, and tuck the cards into a simple holder or a small frame so drinks stay off them.
