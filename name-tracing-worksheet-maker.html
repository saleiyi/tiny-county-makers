Three steps

How to make a name tracing worksheet

Type one name or paste a class list

Put one name on each line. A single name is repeated across every practice row on the page, which is what a child needs; a list gives each child a worksheet of their own so the batch prints in register order.

Pick the rows, the lettering and the lines

Choose two to ten practice rows, up to six blank rows for free writing, and the dotted, dashed, hollow, light grey or solid lettering. Blue red blue school paper, grey ruled paper, a single writing line or no lines at all, in the colour the child is learning to write in.

What makes a good tracing worksheet

  • One name per line is the point of a tracing worksheet. The child copies the same letter shapes over and over, which is what builds the muscle memory.
  • Dotted letters are the classic first step, because the child traces over the dots and the shape is already there. Dashed letters give a little more independence and hollow letters are the next step up.
  • Light grey letters are the gentlest guide of all and are popular for younger children, and solid letters are useful as a wall strip or a name card to copy from.
  • A guided example row gives the child one clean model at the top of the page before the tracing rows start.
  • Blank rows at the foot let the child try the name with no guide at all once the tracing rows are done.

What blue red blue lines mean

  • The blue headline at the top marks where tall letters like b, d, h and l reach.
  • The red dashed midline in the middle is where small letters like a, c, e, m and n sit, and where the top of a tall letter passes.
  • The blue baseline at the bottom is where every letter rests.
  • A single baseline is the simpler option for a first worksheet or for older children, and turning the lines off entirely gives a clean page for free writing.

Printing and classroom use

  • Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is the normal choice, and 120 gsm takes felt tip and crayon without bleeding through.
  • Print at 100 percent with no page scaling, and pick the paper size that matches the tray.
  • Paste a whole class list and each child gets a worksheet of their own, in the order you typed, so a register can be printed and handed out in minutes.
  • A name worksheet works well beside the coloring page maker, the bookmark maker and the place card maker for a classroom or party set.

Questions

Name tracing worksheet FAQ

How do I make a name tracing worksheet?

Type the name you want to practise, or paste a whole class list with one name on each line. Pick how many practice rows you need, choose the dotted, dashed, hollow or solid lettering, choose the blue red blue school lines, and download the sheet as a 300 DPI PNG. The preview and the download are drawn from the same recipe, so what you see on screen is what comes out of the printer.

Is the name tracing worksheet generator free?

Yes. Typing names, changing the row count, the lettering style, the guide lines, the colour and the paper size, and downloading the worksheet as a 300 DPI PNG are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no limit. Nothing you type leaves your device, so a whole class list stays private.

What are blue and red lines on a name tracing worksheet?

Blue red blue paper is the three line handwriting guide a lot of schools use: a blue headline at the top, a dashed red midline in the middle and a blue baseline at the bottom. The top and base lines show where tall letters start and stop, and the red midline shows where small letters like a, c and e sit. It is the ruled paper parents and teachers ask for by name, and it is the default here.

How many names can I put on one worksheet?

One worksheet holds a single name repeated across each writing line, which is how a child practises letter shapes. If you paste a list instead, the tool gives each name its own page and pages through them in the order you typed, so a class list of thirty prints as thirty worksheets. You can add up to forty names in one batch.

What paper and printer should I use?

Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is the normal choice for a practice sheet, and 120 gsm takes felt tip and crayon without bleeding through. Set the paper size to US Letter or A4 to match the tray, print at 100 percent with no page scaling, and pick the highest quality setting so the dotted letters stay crisp enough to trace.

Can I edit the worksheet or use it in cursive?

Everything is editable before you download: retype a name, change the case to uppercase, lowercase or title case, switch between dotted, dashed, hollow, light grey and solid lettering, and turn the guide lines on or off. The sheets print in graphite, blue, green, violet or black so a teacher can match a school handwriting colour.

Can I sell or share the worksheets I make?

Yes. The worksheets you make are yours to print for your own child, your classroom or your tutoring group, and a teacher may print a whole class set. You do not need to credit the site, and there is nothing to sign up for.
