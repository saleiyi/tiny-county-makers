What makes a good tracing worksheet

  • One name per line is the point of a tracing worksheet. The child copies the same letter shapes over and over, which is what builds the muscle memory.
  • Dotted letters are the classic first step, because the child traces over the dots and the shape is already there. Dashed letters give a little more independence and hollow letters are the next step up.
  • Light grey letters are the gentlest guide of all and are popular for younger children, and solid letters are useful as a wall strip or a name card to copy from.
  • A guided example row gives the child one clean model at the top of the page before the tracing rows start.
  • Blank rows at the foot let the child try the name with no guide at all once the tracing rows are done.

What blue red blue lines mean

  • The blue headline at the top marks where tall letters like b, d, h and l reach.
  • The red dashed midline in the middle is where small letters like a, c, e, m and n sit, and where the top of a tall letter passes.
  • The blue baseline at the bottom is where every letter rests.
  • A single baseline is the simpler option for a first worksheet or for older children, and turning the lines off entirely gives a clean page for free writing.

  • Print at 100 percent with no page scaling, and pick the paper size that matches the tray.
