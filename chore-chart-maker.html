Which week to pick

  • Monday to Sunday is the seven column chart for a holiday routine, a summer week or a chart that runs on its own without school. Seven boxes is the widest grid, so the columns are the narrowest of the three and a longer chore line still fits beside them.
  • Monday to Friday is the five column school week. It is the busiest version of the chart, because the weekend jobs are dropped and the boxes grow, which suits a morning routine where the same five things happen before the school bus every day.
  • Monday to Saturday is the six column week that keeps Saturday for the jobs that only happen once, such as changing the beds or washing the car, without leaving a Sunday column to fill.
  • The row count runs from three to fourteen. Fewer rows make each line taller and easier to read, which is what a younger child needs, and more rows fit the longer list a teenager can carry.

Chore chart, reward chart or behaviour chart

  • A chore chart is a record: one job per row, one box per day, ticked when the job is done. It answers the question of what still needs doing this week.
  • A reward chart points the same grid at a goal. Keep the last row for the treat that is earned when the rest of the row is full, or rename the rows after the behaviour you want to see more of.
  • A behaviour chart usually wants the star style, because a row of stars is easy for a child to count, and a short list of three or four rows so the goal stays in sight.
  • A routine chart is the morning and bedtime version: the same five or six steps in the order they happen, printed once and kept where the routine runs.
  • The plain style drops the boxes altogether, which leaves the row free for a sticker, a stamp or a hand drawn smiley.

Printing and reusing the chart

  • Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is fine for a single week. Step up to 160 to 200 gsm card stock for a sheet that will live on a fridge door and be handled every day.
  • Laminate the chart and hand over a dry wipe marker if the same jobs repeat week after week, because the same sheet then covers a whole term instead of a whole week.
  • Print a blank template by clearing the chores box: the day headings and the empty rows still print, ready to write in by hand.

Chore chart maker FAQ

How do I make a chore chart?

Type the jobs into the chores box, one per line or separated by commas, then set the week, the number of rows and the check box style. Add a title, a name and a reward line if you want them printed, pick a colour kit and a paper size, and the preview updates as you type. When the sheet looks right, download the 300 DPI PNG and print it at 100 percent with no page scaling.

Is this chore chart maker free?

Yes. Typing the jobs, switching the week, the rows, the style and the colour kit, and downloading the finished chart as a 300 DPI PNG are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no limit on how many charts you print, and the sheet is drawn inside your browser so nothing you type is uploaded.

How many chores should a chore chart have?

A young child does best with three to five jobs, a primary school child with five to eight, and a teenager can carry ten or more. The chart holds up to fourteen rows, but a shorter list is the one that actually gets finished, so start with the jobs that matter most and add to it once the week runs clean. Any row you leave empty still prints as a blank line to write on by hand.

What is the difference between a chore chart and a reward chart?

A chore chart lists the jobs and a box for each day, so the week is a record of what was done. A reward chart points the same grid at a goal instead: one star per kind act, one tick per bedtime without a fuss, or the last row kept for the treat that is earned when the row is full. The same sheet does both here, because the reward line prints above the last row and the check boxes are the same tick or star boxes.

Can I use this as a behaviour chart?

Yes. Set the week to Monday to Sunday, keep the rows short and name each row after the routine you are working on, such as staying in bed, using kind words or getting ready without a reminder. The star style suits a behaviour chart because a full row of stars is easy for a child to read at a glance, and the plain style with no boxes suits a chart that gets a sticker instead.

Can I make one chart for several children?

Yes, download one chart per child. Type the name in the name box so each sheet is headed with the right child, then change the name and download again. Keeping the jobs identical and the names different makes it easy to run the same week for two or three children, and each download is a full sheet of its own.

What paper should I print a chore chart on?

Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper prints the chart perfectly well, but 160 to 200 gsm card stock is worth the small cost if the sheet will live on a fridge or a bedroom wall. Laminate it and hand over a dry wipe marker if you want the same chart reused every week instead of printed again.

Can I print a blank chore chart template?

Yes. Clear the chores box and the chart prints as a blank template, with the day headings across the top and empty lines down the side to write in by hand. Keep the tick boxes on for a reusable sheet, or switch to the plain style when you would rather use stickers.
