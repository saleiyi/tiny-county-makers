How to make a printable monogram

What the styles print

  • Classic three initials sets the middle letter larger. Type whole names and the surname is moved into that middle spot the traditional way, while initials typed in the order you want are printed as they were written. More than three names are trimmed to the first three.
  • Two initials keeps the ampersand between the letters, which is the wedding and anniversary arrangement most people are after.
  • Single initial centres one letter, which is the version that stays legible at small sizes on a tag, a keyring or a glass.
  • Block four initials sets one letter from each of up to four names in an even, firm row, which keeps a whole name in order instead of rearranging it.
  • Wedding script keeps letters, digits, an ampersand and a full stop, so a couple’s two names print in a joined hand.
  • Number date keeps only the digits and groups up to eight of them, which turns 09 16 2026 into a clean wedding or anniversary date.

Where a monogram gets used

A monogram shows up wherever something is meant to be personal but not illustrated: the front of a wedding invitation, the back of a gift tag, a folded napkin, a plain cushion, a doormat stencil, a wine glass, a phone case, a notebook cover, a tile for a kitchen splashback and a piece of etched glass. The SVG is the export for anything that gets cut or etched, because the frame arrives as one closed path rather than an outline a machine has to guess. The PNG is the export for paper, card and transfer sheets, because it already carries the frame fill and the lettering at a flat 300 DPI.

Making the initials fit the frame

The letters start at the largest size the frame will hold and shrink until the row fits inside it with the frame’s own proportions kept, so nothing spills past the outline and nothing is printed outside the safe border. Two long names in the script style will come out smaller than a single initial, which is what makes one tool usable for both a tiny tag and a large framed piece. If a monogram is going onto something small, the single initial is the one that stays readable.

Questions

Monogram maker FAQ

How do I make a monogram?

Type the initials you want and the frame is redrawn in the browser as you go. Pick a style, a frame and a colour kit, choose US Letter or A4, then download the 300 DPI PNG and print it at 100 percent with no page scaling, or take the SVG when a cutting machine has to follow the frame outline. The whole thing takes under a minute, and nothing you type leaves your device.

Is this monogram maker free?

Yes. Every style, all five frames, all eight colour kits, the PNG and the SVG are free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many you make. There is nothing to buy afterwards, and the monogram is drawn inside your browser rather than on a server.

Which initial goes in the middle of a monogram?

In a traditional three letter monogram the surname goes in the middle and is set larger, with the first name on the left and the middle name on the right. Amelia Rose Bennett becomes A B R, not A R B. Type the whole names and the classic three initial style does that on its own, lifting and enlarging the middle letter for you. Type the initials already in the order you want and they print as they were written.

Which monogram style should I choose?

Classic three initials suits a personal monogram and lifts the surname in the middle. Two initials with an ampersand is the one to reach for on a wedding or anniversary piece. Single initial is the simplest and the best fit for a small frame, a napkin or a keyring. Block four initials sets a whole name in a firm, modern row, wedding script joins a couple's names in a flowing hand, and the number style turns a wedding date or an anniversary into a clean set of digits.

What is the difference between the circle, oval, diamond, shield and square frames?

The circle is the classic monogram frame and the safest choice for any initials. The oval gives a taller, softer shape that suits a two letter wedding monogram. The diamond is a four point lozenge, which is the most formal of the five. The crest shield has a straight top, square shoulders and a taper to a point, the shape you see on family crests. The clipped corner square reads as a plaque rather than a plain box and is the one that fits a square gift tag or a tile.

What size is the printed monogram?

The frame is drawn as the biggest version of the shape that still keeps a real paper border, which works out at about 14 x 14 cm for a circle on US Letter and about 13.5 x 13.5 cm on A4 at 300 DPI, with the taller frames running to roughly 17.6 cm high on Letter. The exact measurement is printed in the preview readout above before you download, so the sheet in your hand measures what the screen promised.

Can I use the monogram with a cutting machine?

Yes. The SVG export carries the frame as one closed path on its own cutline layer, with the artwork on a separate layer above it, which is what Cricut, Silhouette and other cutters follow. Take the SVG for vinyl, iron on, etched glass or a stencil, and the PNG when you are printing on paper, card or a transfer sheet. Both exports are free.

What paper should I print a monogram on?

Plain copy paper is fine for a draft and for a paper craft, card stock gives a printed monogram the weight to stand up on a table, and a matte photo or heavy presentation paper is the best choice for a framed piece. Print at 100 percent with no page scaling, because scaling the sheet down shrinks the frame and the initials with it.
