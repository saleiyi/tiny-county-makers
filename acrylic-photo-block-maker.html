Reference

Acrylic photo block size guide

A photo block is bought by its face size, not by its longest edge alone, because the block is a solid piece of acrylic rather than a print. The maker keeps the proportions of each standard size and crops your photo to fill the face, so what you see is what the block will look like from the front.

What makes a good acrylic photo block

  • Start with a sharp photo. The block crops to its face size, so a soft or heavily zoomed photo shows immediately at 5x7 and 8x10.
  • Keep the subject near the middle. Square attention on 2x2 and 4x4, and leave a little headroom on 5x7 and 8x10 so nothing important lands on the acrylic edge.
  • Check the resolution line under the preview. It tells you the finished size and the pixel count so you know the file really is 300 DPI before you print.
  • Use a portrait photo for 5x7 and 8x10 and a square crop for 2x2 and 4x4. A wide landscape photo loses its sides on a portrait block.
  • Print the PNG on photo paper at the listed size if you want a paper version, or send the same file to an acrylic supplier if you would rather have it made to order.

Acrylic photo block FAQ

How do I make an acrylic photo block online?

Upload a JPG, PNG or WEBP up to 12 MB, choose 2x2, 4x4, 5x7 or 8x10 inches, and the maker frames the photo on the front face with the acrylic edge and depth drawn in. You can then download a transparent 300 DPI PNG for free, or send the photo through the quote form and let us make the block.

What do the 2x2, 4x4, 5x7 and 8x10 sizes mean?

They are the face sizes of the block in inches. 2x2 and 4x4 are square, while 5x7 and 8x10 are portrait, and the maker always frames the photo to the longest side. A 5x7 block exports at 2100 pixels on the long side and an 8x10 block exports at 3000 pixels, both at 300 DPI.

Is the acrylic photo block maker free?

Yes. Uploading a photo, switching between the four sizes and downloading the 300 DPI PNG are free, with no account and no watermark, because everything runs in your browser. A finished acrylic photo block is an optional paid service.

What photo works best in a photo block?

Use a sharp, well-lit photo and keep the main subject near the centre, because the block crops the photo to its face size. Portrait photos suit 5x7 and 8x10, square crops suit 2x2 and 4x4, and a photo of roughly 1500 pixels or more on the long side stays crisp when the block is printed at 300 DPI.

Can I order the physical acrylic photo block?

Yes. Send the photo, the size and roughly how many you need through the quote form, and we reply with the exact finish and the price for that size before anything is made. You approve a proof first, and a PayPal invoice is sent only after you are happy with the design.
