Four steps

How to make a printable paper crown

Type the name for the front of the crown

The name is printed in the middle of the band on both bands, so a crown can be made for one child or two. Leave it empty for a crown that anyone can wear, or type a word like Birthday so the same sheet works for the whole party.

Pick a style, a band depth and a colour

King draws sharp five sided points, queen rounds them into arches, princess cuts hearts, birthday cuts a row of scallops and plain keeps the simplest zigzag, which is the one to choose when a class is cutting thirty crowns out with safety scissors. A slim band suits a small head, a tall band carries a longer name, and the eight colour kits run from rainbow down to plain black and white.

Choose the paper and download the sheet

A crown band runs along the long edge of the page, so the sheet always prints in landscape. US Letter and A4 each give two bands with a 1.7 cm glue tab at the end of every band. Download the 300 DPI PNG and print at 100 percent with no page scaling, so the band measures what the preview promised.

Cut out both bands and join them

Cut along the printed outline, then overlap the tab at the end of one band behind the end of the other and glue or tape it down. One sheet makes roughly 50 cm of crown, which fits most children; print a second sheet for an adult or a larger head. Card stock or a laminated sheet keeps the points standing up through a party.

A paper crown is the one party hat that gets worn twice, because it is cheap enough to make one for everybody and personal enough that nobody wants to hand it back. The template here is drawn rather than downloaded: the points, the band and the name are all measured against the real page, so the band that comes out of the printer is the band that was on screen. How much crown does a head need

  • Babies and toddlers run from about 44 cm to 48 cm around. One sheet is more than enough; overlap the two bands well past the tab and trim the surplus so the points stay evenly spaced.
  • Children are usually between 48 cm and 53 cm, which is exactly what one sheet makes once the 1.7 cm tab is overlapped. This is the size the default settings are drawn for.
  • Teenagers and adults run from about 55 cm to 59 cm, so print a second sheet and join three bands. Overlap each tab fully and the third band takes the crown past 70 cm, which is enough for a large head or a hat worn over hair.
  • Measure before you print if the crown is for a specific person: run a tape measure around the head just above the ears and across the middle of the forehead, and keep the tape level all the way round.

Which crown style to print

  • King is the tall pointed crown people picture first. The points are five sided rather than plain triangles, so they still read as a crown when the band is coloured in by hand.
  • Queen rounds every point into an arch, which is the style that suits a grown up costume as well as a child, and it is the default here.
  • Princess cuts a row of hearts. It takes the longest to cut out but it is the one asked for by name, and it works for Valentine parties as well as birthdays.
  • Birthday cuts scallops, which read as a party hat rather than a royal crown, and it is the fastest of the four shaped styles to cut.
  • Plain keeps a straight zigzag. Choose it when a whole class is cutting, when the crown has to survive being folded into a school bag, or when the band is going to be decorated afterwards.

Getting a crown that survives the party

  • Plain copy paper is fine for a single afternoon. Card stock holds the points up for longer, and a laminated sheet will come back out of the box for years.
  • Glue sticks work on copy paper, tape works on everything, and a stapled tab is the fastest fix when a crown has to be resized on the day.
  • Score the fold line where the tab meets the band before you bend it, and the band will sit flat against the head instead of creasing in the middle of the name.
  • Print the black and white kit and let children colour the band in before it is cut out. It saves a full set of colour ink and it keeps everybody busy for twenty minutes.

Questions

Paper crown maker FAQ

How do I make a paper crown?

Type the name you want on the front, pick a style and a colour, and download the sheet. Print it in landscape on US Letter or A4 at 100 percent with no page scaling, cut along the printed outline, then overlap the glue tab at the end of one band behind the end of the other and fix it with glue or tape. Each sheet carries two bands, which join into roughly 50 cm of crown, and the whole thing takes about five minutes.

Is this crown template free?

Yes. Every style, every band depth, all eight colour kits and the 300 DPI PNG download are free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many crowns you print. The crown is drawn inside your browser, so the name you type never leaves your device.

What size crown fits a child?

A child head is usually between 48 cm and 53 cm around, and one printed sheet makes roughly 50 cm of crown once the glue tab is overlapped, so a single sheet fits most children from about three years old. Measure around the head just above the ears and across the forehead, print a second sheet if the measurement is over 52 cm, and overlap the two sheets as far as you need before gluing.

Which crown style should I print?

King cuts sharp five sided points and suits a birthday king or a school play. Queen rounds each point into an arch and is the softer, more grown up option. Princess cuts a row of hearts, which is the one most often asked for by name. Birthday cuts a row of scallops that reads as a party hat rather than a royal crown, and plain keeps a straight zigzag, which is the quickest to cut when a whole class is making one.

Can I put a name on the crown?

Yes. The name box prints the name in the middle of the band on both bands, and the type shrinks until it fits, so Amelia and Happy Birthday both land on the sheet without spilling over the edges. Leave the box empty for a plain crown that anyone can wear, or print the same sheet twice with two different names.

What paper should I print the crown on?

Plain copy paper works and folds easily, but the points flop after a while. Card stock holds the shape through a party, and a laminated sheet holds it for a classroom set that gets used every year. Print at 100 percent with no page scaling, because scaling the sheet down shrinks the band and the crown stops fitting the head it was measured for.

How do I join the two bands together?

Each band ends in a white tab marked with a dashed outline and a fold line. Cut along the solid outline of the band, overlap that tab behind the plain end of the other band, and glue or tape it down. If the crown is too big, overlap further than the tab and trim the extra length; if it is too tight, print a second sheet and add a third band.

Can I print the crown in black and white?

Yes. The last colour kit is plain black and white, which prints a solid dark band with white lettering and costs nothing in colour ink. It is the version to choose for a whole class, and children can colour the band in before it is cut out. The other seven kits tint the band and the cutting outline, so the finished crown keeps its colour on any printer.
