How much crown does a head need

  • Babies and toddlers run from about 44 cm to 48 cm around. One sheet is more than enough; overlap the two bands well past the tab and trim the surplus so the points stay evenly spaced.
  • Children are usually between 48 cm and 53 cm, which is exactly what one sheet makes once the 1.7 cm tab is overlapped. This is the size the default settings are drawn for.
  • Teenagers and adults run from about 55 cm to 59 cm, so print a second sheet and join three bands. Overlap each tab fully and the third band takes the crown past 70 cm, which is enough for a large head or a hat worn over hair.
  • Measure before you print if the crown is for a specific person: run a tape measure around the head just above the ears and across the middle of the forehead, and keep the tape level all the way round.

Which crown style to print

  • King is the tall pointed crown people picture first. The points are five sided rather than plain triangles, so they still read as a crown when the band is coloured in by hand.
  • Queen rounds every point into an arch, which is the style that suits a grown up costume as well as a child, and it is the default here.
  • Princess cuts a row of hearts. It takes the longest to cut out but it is the one asked for by name, and it works for Valentine parties as well as birthdays.
  • Birthday cuts scallops, which read as a party hat rather than a royal crown, and it is the fastest of the four shaped styles to cut.
  • Plain keeps a straight zigzag. Choose it when a whole class is cutting, when the crown has to survive being folded into a school bag, or when the band is going to be decorated afterwards.

Getting a crown that survives the party

  • Plain copy paper is fine for a single afternoon. Card stock holds the points up for longer, and a laminated sheet will come back out of the box for years.
  • Glue sticks work on copy paper, tape works on everything, and a stapled tab is the fastest fix when a crown has to be resized on the day.
  • Score the fold line where the tab meets the band before you bend it, and the band will sit flat against the head instead of creasing in the middle of the name.
  • Print the black and white kit and let children colour the band in before it is cut out. It saves a full set of colour ink and it keeps everybody busy for twenty minutes.
