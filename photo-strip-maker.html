Photo strip sizes, frames and paper

A booth strip is a tall, narrow print, so the two numbers in its name describe the paper it is cut from rather than the strip on its own. Both layouts here export at 300 DPI, which is what a photo lab expects.

Choosing the paper colour

  • White is the standard lab print and the safest choice for colour photos.
  • Black makes the frames read like a darkroom strip; the caption switches to white so it stays readable.
  • Cream suits weddings, anniversaries and anything with a warm or vintage palette.
  • Blush is a soft pink that works for birthdays, hen parties and baby showers.
  • Any other colour is fine too: the swatch picker takes any hex value and the caption ink flips automatically at mid grey.

What the download contains

  • A flat 300 DPI PNG of the finished strip, paper included, at the exact print size you chose.
  • Three or four frames with even gutters, cropped to fill rather than squashed or letterboxed.
  • The caption band at the foot of the strip, shrunk to fit if the text runs long.
  • No watermark, no logo and no signup, so the file goes straight to a lab or a home printer.

Printing tips

  • Ask the lab for a 2 x 6 in print with no border, or a 4 x 6 in print for the two-strip sheet.
  • A home photo printer is fine: set the paper size, turn off auto-correct, and print at the highest quality.
  • Keep the strip away from direct sun. Dye prints fade; a frame or a sleeve slows that down a lot.
  • Printing on matte paper? It suits black and cream strips, while gloss keeps colour photos punchier.
  • Want a matching extra? The same photos work in the photo keychain maker or as a fridge magnet.

Questions

Photo strip FAQ

How do I make a photo strip?

Upload three or four photos, choose whether the strip carries three frames or four, pick the paper colour and type an optional caption. The preview redraws as you change anything, and the download gives you a print-ready 300 DPI PNG of the strip.

Is the photo strip maker free?

Yes. Uploading photos, changing the layout, the paper colour and the caption, and downloading the PNG are all free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many strips you make. Everything runs in your browser, so the photos are never uploaded to us.

What size is a photo booth strip?

A classic booth strip is 2 x 6 in, about 5 x 15 cm, which is why the download is a 300 DPI PNG at 600 x 1800 px. Choose the 4 x 6 in layout when you want two identical strips on one photo print, then cut straight down the middle.

How many photos go on a strip?

Three or four. Four frames is the classic booth look and is what most people expect, while three leaves a little more room in each frame for tall or wide subjects and keeps the caption band comfortable.

Can I print the strip at a photo lab?

Yes. The PNG is a standard 2 x 6 in photo print at 300 DPI, so any photo lab, kiosk or home printer that handles 2 x 6 in prints will take it. The 4 x 6 in layout is the ordinary 4 x 6 in photo print with two strips side by side.
