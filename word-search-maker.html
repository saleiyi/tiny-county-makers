Three steps

How to make a printable word search puzzle

Type or paste your word list

Put one word on each line, or paste a comma separated list, or tap a ready-made theme such as animals, school or Halloween and edit it afterwards. Duplicates are folded away and anything longer than the grid can hold is reported rather than silently dropped.

Choose the grid, the difficulty and the letter case

Auto sizes the square to the words you entered. Easy hides words across and down, medium adds a diagonal, and hard uses all eight directions including reversed spellings. Uppercase is the classroom default, lowercase suits early readers, and the title prints at the top of the sheet.

Download the puzzle and the answer key

Tick the answer key and every placed word is highlighted, so a teacher can print one solved copy and hand out clean sheets. Download a 300 DPI PNG on US Letter or A4 and print at 100 percent with no page scaling, and the letters come out the size they were on screen.

Which grid size to pick

  • 10 x 10 is the quick puzzle: five to eight short words, finished in a few minutes, and the right size for a five to seven year old.
  • 12 x 12 and 15 x 15 are the classroom sizes, and they hold a spelling list or a vocabulary set of ten to twenty words without the words crowding each other.
  • 18 x 18 is for a long list on hard, where twenty or more words have to cross each other and the grid has to stay readable in print.
  • Auto reads the longest word and the length of your list and picks the smallest square that still seats everything, which is the safe choice when you are not sure.

What the difficulty levels change

  • Easy hides words left to right and top to bottom only. Nothing is reversed and nothing runs on a diagonal, which is the puzzle a beginner can finish alone.
  • Medium adds the top left to bottom right diagonal. It is the step up a lot of teachers reach for once a child is comfortable with straight lines.
  • Hard uses all eight directions, so words read upwards, right to left and along both diagonals. Reversed spellings are what make a familiar word take a second pass to find.
  • The shuffle button rebuilds the same word list in a fresh layout with the same settings, which is how you print a different sheet for each child without retyping anything.

Printing and classroom use

  • Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is the normal choice; a heavier 120 gsm takes felt tip without the letters bleeding through to the next sheet.
  • Print one copy with the answer key ticked for yourself and then print the class set with it switched off.
  • Paste a class register as the word list and the children's own names go into the puzzle, which is a lot more engaging than a generic word bank.
  • A word search sits well beside the name tracing worksheet maker, the coloring page maker and the bookmark maker for a classroom or party pack.

Word search maker FAQ

How do I make a word search puzzle?

Type or paste the words you want hidden, one per line, and the tool sizes a square grid to fit them. Each word is hidden across, down or diagonally depending on the difficulty you chose, and every empty square is filled with a random letter so the puzzle cannot be read off the gaps. Download the 300 DPI PNG and print it at 100 percent.

Is this word search maker free?

Yes. The word search maker is free, needs no account and adds no watermark. The puzzle is built in your browser, so nothing you type is uploaded and there is no per-word or per-download charge.

Can I use my own spelling list?

Yes. Paste any spelling list, vocabulary list, class register or party guest list, one word per line or separated by commas. Duplicates are folded away automatically and up to twenty-four words go into one puzzle, which covers a normal week's spelling list with room to spare.

How do I print the answer key?

Tick the answer key box and every placed word is highlighted with a soft colour band behind the letters, so the solution is obvious without the grid becoming unreadable. Print one highlighted copy for the teacher, then switch it back off for the sheets the children get.

What grid size should I choose?

Leave it on auto for most puzzles, because it reads the longest word and the length of your list and picks the smallest square that still seats everything. A 10 x 10 grid suits a short list of five to eight words, 12 x 12 or 15 x 15 suits a classroom spelling list, and 18 x 18 only makes sense when you have twenty or more words on hard.

Can I make a word search for my class?

Yes. Paste the whole class list, or a themed vocabulary list, and press shuffle for a different layout of the same words with the same settings. That is what turns one sheet into a set for a class rather than a page everyone copies, and you can print one answer key and a different puzzle for each row.

Does it upload my words or my child's name?

No. The puzzle is generated by JavaScript running on your own device, so the word list, the class register and any name you type stay in the browser tab and are never sent to a server. There is no account, no email and nothing to install.

Can I sell or share the puzzles I make?

Yes. The puzzles you make are yours to print for your own child, your classroom, your tutoring group or a party, and a teacher may print a whole class set. You do not need to credit the site, and there is nothing to sign up for.
