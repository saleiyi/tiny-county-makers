Three steps

How to make printable bingo cards

Choose numbers or your own words

Leave the mode on numbers for the classic 1 to 75 game, where every card draws its own five numbers from each of the B, I, N, G and O ranges. Switch to words and type one entry per line for sight words, spelling lists, class names or a party theme, or tap a ready-made theme such as animals, Christmas or baby shower to fill the box.

Set the grid, the layout and the card count

Pick a 5x5, 4x4 or 3x3 grid, then choose one, two or four cards on a sheet and how many cards the whole set needs. Two or four to a page saves card stock, the free square can be switched on or off, and every card in the set is generated as a different arrangement so no two players hold the same sheet.

Download the cards and the caller sheet

Take a 300 DPI PNG on US Letter or A4, tick the caller sheet for the extra page of every number or word in the round, and print at 100 percent with no page scaling. The cards come out at the size they were on screen, and there is no watermark, no signup and nothing to install.

Bingo works because every card is different and every card is fair, and that is exactly the part that is slow to build by hand. A 5x5 card draws five numbers from each of the B, I, N, G and O ranges, so the whole 1 to 75 pool is spread evenly instead of clustering, and each card in a set is checked against the others so no two players hold the same arrangement. The same engine also prints word bingo, which is where it earns its keep in a classroom: a week of sight words, a spelling list, the names of the children in the class or a baby shower gift list all turn into a set of cards plus a caller sheet in about a minute.

Which bingo grid to pick

  • 5 x 5 is the classic 75 ball card. The columns run B, I, N, G and O, each column holds five numbers drawn from its own block of fifteen, and the middle square is the free space, which is why a full house is twenty-four numbers rather than twenty-five.
  • 4 x 4 is the sixteen square card used for picture bingo and quick rounds. It has no single centre square, so the free space box does not apply, and it fills up faster than the classic grid, which suits a party rather than a long session.
  • 3 x 3 is the nine square card for young children. Nine squares is a full house a five year old can scan without help, and the middle square can still be a free space, so the card is really eight things to find.
  • The shuffle button rebuilds the same numbers or words into a fresh set of cards with the same settings, which is how you print a different set for the next class without retyping anything.

What the two modes change

  • Numbers is the traditional game: every card picks its own five numbers from each column range, so the pool runs from 1 to 75 and a caller can draw from a hat or a random number generator.
  • Words replaces the numbers with your own entries, printed inside the squares. Use it for sight words, spelling words, vocabulary, the names of the children in the class, Christmas or Halloween vocabulary, or a baby shower list of gifts.
  • The ready-made themes fill the box with a full set of words, and they stay editable afterwards, so a theme is a starting point rather than a fixed list.
  • The caller sheet prints every call in the round in columns to cross off, so the caller does not have to read numbers back off the cards as the game runs.

Printing and classroom use

  • Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is enough for a single game. Step up to 160 to 200 gsm card stock if players will use a pen, a sticker or a dauber, because wet ink bleeds through thin paper.
  • Put two or four cards on a sheet when the set is large, then cut along the card edges. Four to a page is the cheapest way to run a party or a whole class.
  • Laminate a set and hand out dry wipe markers if you want to reuse the same cards every week instead of printing a new set each time.
  • A bingo set sits well beside the word search maker, the name tracing worksheet maker and the coloring page maker for a classroom or party pack.

Questions

Bingo card maker FAQ

How do I make a bingo card?

Tell the tool what should go inside the squares: leave it on numbers for the classic 1 to 75 game, or switch to words and type one entry per line. Then pick the grid, how many cards the set needs and the paper size, and download a 300 DPI PNG. Every card in the set is built as a different arrangement, so a class never ends up with two matching sheets.

Is this bingo card maker free?

Yes. Choosing the grid, the layout, the card count and the letter case, and downloading the sheets as 300 DPI PNGs are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no limit on how many sets you print, and the cards are drawn inside your browser so nothing you type is uploaded.

What is the difference between 3x3, 4x4 and 5x5 bingo?

A 5x5 card is the classic 75 ball game: five columns headed B, I, N, G and O, each drawing five numbers from its own range of fifteen, with a free square in the middle. A 4x4 card is the sixteen square version used for picture bingo and quick rounds, and a 3x3 card is the nine square game that suits young children, because nine squares is a full house a five year old can hold in their head.

Can I use my own words instead of numbers?

Yes. Switch the mode to words and type one entry per line, or paste a comma separated list. Sight words, spelling lists, class registers, baby shower gifts, Christmas words, Halloween characters and travel bingo are the usual uses, and the words print inside the card in place of the numbers. A card needs nine, sixteen or twenty-five entries depending on the grid you picked, and themes such as animals or sight words fill the box in one tap.

What is the free space in the middle?

A 5x5 and a 3x3 card have an odd number of squares, so the centre square can be a free space that every player marks before the first number is called. Tick the free square box to print it with a soft gold fill, or untick it to leave a normal number or word in the middle. A 4x4 card has no single centre square, so it never shows a free space.

How many cards can I make at once?

Up to thirty cards in one set. Two cards on a sheet suits a small group that shares a page, and four cards on a sheet saves card stock for a party, so the layout is worth setting before you download. A set that runs longer than one sheet pages through with the sheet control, and each sheet downloads as its own 300 DPI PNG so you can print them in order.

Can I print a list of the calls for the caller?

Yes. Tick the caller sheet box and one extra sheet is added to the download with every number or word in the set, laid out in columns the caller can cross off as the game runs. In number mode the caller sheet is the shuffled 1 to 75 sequence for that round; in word mode it is your own list, printed in the order you typed it.

What paper should I print bingo cards on?

Plain 80 to 100 gsm copy paper is fine for a single game, but 160 to 200 gsm card stock is worth the small cost because players mark the sheet with a pen, a sticker or a dauber. Print at 100 percent with no page scaling so the squares stay the size they were on screen, and laminate the cards if you want to reuse them with a dry wipe marker.
