How many tags fit on a sheet

  • A US Letter sheet (21.6 x 27.9 cm) and an A4 sheet (21 x 29.7 cm) each hold 9 two by three inch, 6 two and a half by three and a half inch or 4 three by four inch tags.
  • The tool leaves an 8 mm printer border and a 2.5 mm gutter between tags, and draws a light dashed line down each gutter so a trimmer can separate the whole run in a few passes.
  • Both paper sizes fit the same number because the tag is small next to the paper, so pick whichever one your printer loads.
  • The sheet download carries a punch guide at every tag, so the whole batch can be punched in one pass after it is cut apart.

The four silhouettes

  • Classic tag is the shop-bought shape, with the two shoulders cut back at an angle so the hole sits on its own little neck.
  • Rounded corner is a soft rectangle, which suits a photo and a long message better than the angled tag does.
  • Scalloped edge adds a frilled border that reads as a hand-cut craft card, with the same footprint as the straight tag.
  • Square is the easiest shape to trim with a paper cutter and tiles neatly on a sheet.

Card colours, messages and hanging

  • White is the safe choice for a colour photo because the card does not tint the picture.
  • Ivory and blush suit weddings, anniversaries and a soft pastel palette.
  • Sage works for a woodland, botanical or baby shower theme, and kraft looks like unbleached card stock tied with twine.
  • Black makes the message switch to white type, which reads well over a dark or high-contrast photo.
  • The message and the To and From lines sit on the card below the hole, so they stay readable over any photo. Keep the message under about 18 characters on the smallest tag and longer messages will shrink to fit.
  • The dashed circle shows where the eyelet goes. A 3 mm or 1/8 inch punch or an eyelet setter is the right tool, and an eyelet stops kraft or recycled card tearing out against the string.
  • Thread baker's twine, ribbon or jute through the hole and tie it to the gift, or use a small ball chain for a bottle or a kraft box.

Printing and finishing

  • Print the sheet at 100 percent on 200-300 gsm card stock, on the highest quality setting your printer offers.
  • Matte stock soaks up ink and suits the ivory, kraft and blush cards; gloss keeps a colour photo punchier.
  • Trim along the dashed line rather than the solid edge, so no cut mark is left on the printed card.

Questions

Gift tag FAQ

How do I make a gift tag?

Upload a photo, or skip the photo and print a blank card to write on, then pick a 2 x 3, 2.5 x 3.5 or 3 x 4 inch size and one of the four silhouettes. Type a message and the To and From lines if you want them, and download the 300 DPI PNG. The preview and the download are drawn from the same recipe, so what you see on screen is what comes out of the printer.

Is the gift tag maker free?

Yes. Uploading a photo, changing the size, the shape, the card colour, the font, the message and the To and From lines, and downloading a single tag or a whole Letter or A4 sheet as a 300 DPI PNG are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no limit, and the photo never leaves your device.

What size should a gift tag be?

Two by three inches (5.1 x 7.6 cm) is the size most shops sell and it suits a small box, a favour bag or a jewellery gift. Two and a half by three and a half inches (6.4 x 8.9 cm) is the classic stationery tag and reads well under a tree, and three by four inches (7.6 x 10.2 cm) gives a group photo or a long message room to breathe. A tag looks right when it is about a third of the present it hangs from.

How many gift tags fit on one sheet?

A US Letter or A4 sheet holds nine 2 x 3 inch tags, six 2.5 x 3.5 inch tags or four 3 x 4 inch tags. The tool leaves an 8 mm printer border and a 2.5 mm gutter between tags, and draws a light dashed line down each gutter so you can separate the whole run with a trimmer.

What paper should I print gift tags on?

200 to 300 gsm card stock is the right weight: heavy enough that the tag does not flop or tear at the hole, thin enough to take a clean cut. Matte stock soaks up ink and suits ivory, kraft and blush cards, gloss keeps a colour photo punchier, and a colour laser keeps the sheet flat instead of curling. Print at 100 percent with no page scaling or the tag will come out a different size than the one you picked.

How do I punch and attach a gift tag?

The dashed circle near the top of the tag marks where the hole goes, so use a 3 mm or 1/8 inch hole punch or an eyelet setter on that mark. A hole with a metal eyelet lasts far longer on kraft or recycled card, because the paper cannot tear out against the string. Thread baker's twine, ribbon, jute or a small ball chain through the hole and tie it to the gift; the string loop is drawn on the preview so you can see how the tag hangs.
