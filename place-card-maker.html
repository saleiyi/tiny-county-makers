Tent cards or flat cards

  • Tent cards fold along the middle and stand on their own, and the name prints the right way up on both faces, so it reads from either side of the table.
  • Flat cards lie down and suit a holder, a folded napkin or a laid place setting, and they fit twice as many to a sheet, so they are the cheapest to print.

Meal choice marks

  • Type the meal after the name, separated by a comma, a pipe or a slash: Sarah Chen, chicken.
  • The tool recognises beef, chicken, fish, the vegetarian option and a kids meal, and prints a small line-art mark under the name.
  • Common synonyms are matched too, so steak, salmon, veggie and child all land on the right mark. Guests with no meal simply print a plain name.

Choosing the paper colour

  • White is the safest stock and matches most stationery suites.
  • Ivory reads warmer than white and suits traditional and autumn palettes.
  • Blush picks up a soft pink scheme without a printed border.
  • Sage works with greenery, dried flowers and modern schemes.
  • Black flips the type to white for an evening look; it is the one to avoid on a home inkjet.

Printing and cutting tips

  • Print at 100 percent, no "fit to page", or the cards come out a fraction small for a bought holder.
  • Use 200 gsm card stock or heavier so tent cards stand without bowing.
  • Cut the sheets before folding: straight through each trim line with a guillotine gives the cleanest edges.
  • Crease the tent fold with a bone folder so the card sits flat instead of rocking.
  • Print one sheet first and check the names against the seating plan before you run the whole set.

Place card FAQ

How do I make place cards?

Paste your guest list on the left, one name per line, and add a meal choice after a comma or a pipe if you are offering one. Choose the sheet size and whether the cards fold as tents or stay flat, then download every sheet as a print-ready 300 DPI PNG.

Is the place card maker free?

Yes. Typing the guest list, switching between tent and flat cards, changing the font and the paper colour, and downloading every sheet as a 300 DPI PNG are all free. There is no account, no watermark and no page limit, and nothing you type is uploaded anywhere.

How many place cards fit on one sheet?

A US Letter or A4 sheet takes 10 flat cards at 3.5 x 2 in each, or four folded tent cards at 3.5 x 4 in. The tool paginates the guest list for you, so a list of 60 guests downloads as six flat sheets or fifteen tent sheets.

What is the difference between tent and flat place cards?

A tent card is folded in half so it stands up on its own, with the name printed the right way up on both faces. A flat card lies down, so it sits in a holder or on the plate. Tent cards are the usual pick for a seated dinner; flat cards suit buffets and settings that already carry a menu.

How do I add meal choices to the cards?

Type the meal after the guest name with a comma, a pipe or a slash, like Sarah Chen, chicken or Michael Ross | beef. The tool recognises beef, chicken, fish, the vegetarian option and a kids meal, and prints a small line-art mark under the name. Guests with no meal simply print their name.

Can I print place cards at home?

Yes. Print the sheets at 100 percent on 200 gsm card stock and cut along the light trim lines. A guillotine, or a craft knife and a steel ruler, gives the cleanest edges, and a bone folder makes a crisper fold on the tent cards.
